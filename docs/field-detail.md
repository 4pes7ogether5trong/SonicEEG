# Frequency detail and four-patient layout

The previous renderer stacked five translucent shells separated by only 0.032 head radii, obscuring concurrent activity. The revised renderer uses one contoured, depth-tested surface per time slice. Its color follows the strongest interpolated band, or the selected band. It does not claim greater spatial resolution than the montage supplies.

Channel markers sit on the measured scalp surface; there are no protruding electrode bars. Select an individual frequency band to inspect secondary components. Unavailable channels have hollow gray markers and contribute no amplitude. White rings mark experimental sharp-shape candidates, which can include artifacts; these are not diagnosis labels. Both renderers use the same surface and marker measurements.

Sharp-candidate counts are deduplicated across overlapping analysis windows, reset at pipeline boundaries and retained by time-bin merging. They count observed candidates, with window-dependent latency; they do not measure seizures or clinical event burden. History defaults to retained peaks, with mean power and threshold occupancy still available through Field controls.

Side view has an underlying ruler with Start and Present timestamps and unlabeled intermediate ticks. A short white tick identifies the selected time. The ruler rotates with the scene and follows the same time expansion as the heads. Labels keep a constant screen size in WebGL. The long axis is time, not left-to-right anatomy. Each small head has the same anatomical orientation. Whole history retains the concentric age mapping.

The color menu adds Baseline change and Change prevalence in live, side and whole-history views. Each patient pins an independent two-second reference through Adjust → Pin baseline while fresh measurements arrive. Source, segment or setting changes clear the reference. Synthetic sampling pins the 4–6 second window automatically. Earlier intervals are unavailable for comparison; repinning creates a separate reference identity.

For each band, change is `20 log10((current band RMS + 2 µV)/(reference band RMS + 2 µV))`. The floor bounds the ratio near zero. Blue represents a decrease, orange an increase, with intensity capped at 12 dB. Coarse bins retain the duration-weighted mean signed change. All layers chooses the largest absolute mean band change. A single selected band remains available.

Change prevalence is the fraction of compared valid time at or beyond ±6 dB in the selected band, or any band in All layers. It is accumulated before compression, preserving brief excursions even when signed changes cancel. The denominator excludes pre-reference and unavailable intervals. Bins spanning incompatible reference IDs are unavailable; zoom into an interval to inspect its compatible components. Channel details show the percentage and compared seconds. This is prevalence of a specified change criterion, including sleep and artifact, not abnormality or seizure burden.

The history strip for the new sampler shows programmed synthetic seizure intervals. No inferred seizure probability is calculated. A calibrated probability requires a model validated for its input domain and population; raw EEG alone does not establish that validation. Baseline measurements can operate on calibrated screen-derived traces, with the existing timebase, reference, filtering, missing-channel and pixel-resolution limits. No screenshot is transmitted for this calculation.

The **4 patients** switch moves each patient's existing sound card into its own visual tile: A top left, B top right, C bottom left, D bottom right. This preserves the original independent capture, calibration, montage, sound and history instances; it does not share or remix their measurements. Single-patient view remains available. At narrow widths the layout stacks vertically. The listening exercise returns cards to its existing layout and hides all fields during trials.

Validation covers dominant-band selection, unavailable-channel exclusion, time-axis endpoints and monotonicity across lens settings, and sharp-count preservation through compression. Existing numerical, audio, capture and exercise tests are retained. Browser interaction and physical graphics/audio acceptance remain to be evaluated; these code checks do not establish perceptual clarity or clinical performance.
